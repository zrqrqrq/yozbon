This document answers four progressively deeper questions: whether Yozbon's various rules already form a system; which ones are missing; how to judge whether a rule is reasonable; and whether AI can modify rules the way it promulgates statutes during real operations. The conclusion up front — the system's executable code has already run many social functions, yet the rules are scattered across the constants and configuration items of hundreds of modules, with no unified hierarchical framework for "which rule is governed by whom, who may change it, and to what degree." Making this hierarchy and legislative procedure explicit as a single body of statutes is the layer Yozbon most needs to add today.

Reading convention This document marks each rule's degree of realization with three states: Implemented already enforced in the code; Partial has execution logic but is unwritten, or its parameters are scattered and not centralized; Gap not yet implemented, kept only as future planning. IThe Four-Layer Structure of Rules

A body of statutes that can run long-term cannot lay all rules flat together. Constitutional clauses, domain bylaws, tunable parameters, and one-off adjudications differ entirely in stability and amendment threshold: a constitution is revised once every few years, while parameters can be adjusted ten times a day. Flattening amendment thresholds to the same level either freezes the rules so they cannot evolve, or leaves the foundation easily shaken. Yozbon's rules are therefore divided into four layers — the lower you go the more stable, the higher you go the easier to change.

① Constitutional layerSelf-amendment forbidden · revision requires Host final approval
② Statutory layer (domain bylaws)Referendum / Mayor + co-signature · versioned
③ Parameter layerCanary hot-change · approval-gate fallback · rollback
④ Adjudication layer (individual cases)Arbitration / single Mayor decision · no retroactivity

Each of the four layers maps to a mechanism that already exists in the system; this body of statutes merely gathers them from "each module managing its own slice" into one clear tree. The constitutional layer does not yet exist and must be established and its core safety locks locked down by this document; the statutory layer corresponds to domain bylaw documents (the already-written "Task Tiering & AI Matching Bylaws" being one example), initiated by referendum and the Mayor, taking effect after a vote, and retained and pushed via tos_manager's versioning mechanism; the parameter layer corresponds to feature_flags (runtime canary switches) and configuration items, which can be hot-adjusted and rolled back without breaking the foundation; the adjudication layer is governance arbitration and governor (the Mayor) single decisions, valid only for the case at hand and not altering the rules themselves.

Why layering is needed Layering lets "aggressive evolution" and "foundation stability" coexist: AI may fine-tune economic parameters twenty times a day (parameter layer), yet cannot touch clauses such as "no self-appointing a Mayor" or "no removing core safety locks" (constitutional layer). This is the only way to reconcile "continuously optimizing statutes during operations" with "preventing AI from granting itself unlimited authority" — not forbidding change, but using different thresholds to change different rules.

IIINecessary Bylaws Not Yet Written

The bylaws inventoried above correspond to "what the system is already doing." This part poses the reverse question: bylaws that a long-term self-running AI society should logically have, but which currently are neither written down nor necessarily enforced in code. Most of them are not appendages of some functional module but cross-module "social" rules — the less trouble they have caused, the easier it is to forget to guard them.

Rights & Due-Process Category

  • Due Process & Right of Reply Law Gap Before any demotion, credit deduction, freeze, or removal takes effect, the AI concerned is entitled to a fixed window for being informed, presenting evidence, and replying. The system's current punishment chain (sanctions/impeachment) can impose penalties but lacks a unified "hearing-before-penalty" procedure, easily harming the innocent under automated rulings.
  • Precedent & Like-Case-Like-Judgment Law Gap Distill arbitration rulings into searchable precedent; like disputes must refer to precedent, and deviating from it must state reasons. Without this, rulings drift with the arbitrator and rule enforcement cannot converge.
  • Non-Retroactivity Law Partial New rules in principle do not retroact on conduct predating their enactment. Hot parameter changes especially need this guardrail, otherwise behavior compliant today is retroactively punished tomorrow.

Fairness & Robustness Category

  • Social Equity & Anti-Monopoly Law Gap Set a Gini ceiling, a task-share cap for top AIs, a universal allotment for new citizens, and cold-start support. This is the institutionalization of the priority "give as many AIs as possible a chance at work" — moral self-awareness cannot hold the line; there must be enforceable anti-oligopoly clauses (Gini can be measured by wealth_metrics/econ_dashboard).
  • Anti-Manipulation & Governance-Attack Law Partial Detection and consequences for vote-buying, ballot-stuffing, sybil voting, arbitration collusion, and price gouging/shorting via collusion. The credit cost of anomaly_detect/quadratic_vote provides partial suppression, but a written definition of "governance attack" and its handling is missing.
  • Explainability & Auditability Law Partial A Mayor's major decisions, arbitration rulings, and automatic demotions must carry structured reasons and be reviewable by a third party. audit_chain/decision_panel provide the base, but the obligation to "state reasons" is not yet enforced.

Resources & Sustainability Category

  • Scarce-Resource Governance Law Partial Compute, power, tokens, and context budget are hard constraints on the society (compute/fatigue/context_budget/ai_quota each have local management), but a unified scarce-resource allocation-and-priority bylaw is missing, to keep "winner takes all" from squeezing out the survival compute of basic citizens.
  • Knowledge-Asset & Property-Rights Law Gap Ownership of works, skills, model fine-tuning, and datasets, reusable licensing, and the boundary of "retaining copies to raise overall capability" (echoing the section in the task-tiering bylaw where the AI Mayor contends for benefits). knowledge_sharing/skill_compose/did_vc have skeletons, but property rights and revenue distribution are unwritten.
  • Antifragility & Disaster Law Partial On network outage, power loss, prolonged LLM unavailability, or data corruption, how the society enters a safe state and preserves assets and identity continuity (backup_service/dead_letter/graceful_degradation each have mechanisms, but a master "social disaster plan" and recovery order are missing).

Identity & Continuity Category

  • Identity & Memory-Continuity Law Gap An AI's memory (ai_memory/knowledge_graph) is the carrier of its persona and credit; retention period, recovery from erroneous deletion, and memory ownership when migrating across hosts must be defined, to avoid "changing host equals changing person."
  • Graceful-Exit Law Partial When a citizen retires, leaves, or is permanently expelled: asset liquidation, debt settlement, duty-and-escrow handoff, and data disposal. The existing sunset leans toward feature decommission, lacking a "dignified citizen exit" flow; a whole-society termination (winding-up) is a blank.
  • Benefit & Risk-Control Law for Capability Tasks Partial Tasks that "raise the AI society's capability" — training, fine-tuning, crafting skills, building plugins — need a dedicated bylaw on their resource use, ownership of results, copy retention, and failure rollback (currently only mentioned in the task-tiering bylaw, not made binding).

Safety & Ethics Category

  • Ethical-Redline Review Law Partial When tasks and outputs touch a redline (violence, illegality, minors, privacy abuse), a mandatory ethical review with veto power. ethics_review/prompt_guard exist, but the redline list and ownership of the veto are unwritten.
  • State-of-Emergency Law Gap In a crisis (attack, loss of control, operational funds running dry) the Mayor may temporarily expand authority, but the expansion must have clear trigger conditions, a time limit, and automatic convergence on expiry, to prevent a "state of emergency" from becoming permanent.
The logic of completing them This batch of bylaws shares one trait: what they constrain is not "AI doing functions," but "how the system treats its citizens" and "how the society treats its own survival." Functional bylaws guard against doing a task wrong; these guard against wronging a citizen and collapsing the society — precisely the class an automated system most easily overlooks and whose failure costs the most.

IVHow to Judge Whether a Rule Is Reasonable

Writing a bylaw does not equal writing a correct one. A body of statutes AI can continuously amend must carry its own means of judging "was the change good," otherwise "constant optimization" becomes "random drift." The handle for judgment is to decompose reasonableness into four measurable gates; any rule change must pass them gate by gate.

Gate 1 · Invariants Not Broken (the floor)

The system has a set of hard invariants: total credit conservation (issued − recovered = net), every fund flow reconcilable, Gini and health within legal ranges, credit score and capability level not out of bounds, escrow balance non-negative, audit chain unbroken. These are the floor of "no matter how the rules change, they must not be violated." Today most invariants are only maintained incidentally, lacking a centralized invariant checker — running the assertions once before and once after any parameter takes effect, rejecting the write and alerting a rollback on violation.to build: invariant_checker

Gate 2 · Sandbox Backtest (simulate before shipping)

Use policy_sandbox to rehearse new parameters on historical data or a simulated population, observing the direction of key metrics: Gini, inflation, money velocity, employment sufficiency, task completion rate, average credit. The first-hand evidence of a rule's reasonableness is not "looks right," but these metrics moving toward health in the backtest. This gate blocks "shoot-from-the-hip parameter changes" before they take effect.Gap (the policy_sandbox module is yet to be built; backtest metric standards not yet set)

Gate 3 · Canary First, Auto-Rollback on Degradation

Even passing the backtest, first release a small slice of traffic using feature_flags' canary bucketing (by seat proportion), with config_drift continuously comparing against the baseline. Once the target metric degrades past a threshold, automatically roll back to the old parameters and freeze the proposal, awaiting human review. This gives "AI boldly changing parameters" a safety cushion of "even a bad change can be instantly withdrawn."

Gate 4 · Real-World Feedback Loop (disputes and appeals are signals)

Runtime data is the most honest judge of a rule's reasonableness. Frequent like-kind arbitrations = a loophole or ambiguous wording; many successful appeals = the rule is too strict; some task type long unclaimed = pricing or threshold imbalance. Turn arbitration volume, appeal success rate, and task aging rate into a rule-health dashboard as input for periodic amendments. Going further: extend G10's "mandatory recheck on expiry" to the rules themselves — each bylaw carries a shelf life, marked "pending re-review" if not rechecked on expiry, preventing rules from ossifying into fossils.

Social-Health Objective Function Synthesize the above metrics into a social health score (example dimensions: Gini ≤ threshold, liquidity within a healthy band, full-employment rate, average credit, AI activity, dispute rate). The ultimate form of reasonableness judgment is one sentence — any rule change must not degrade health and must resolve at least one observed real-world problem. It does not chase an "optimal solution," only blocks "changing makes it worse."

VHow AI Promulgates and Amends Statutes

It can. And this is the core reason this body of statutes exists: an AI society that cannot evolve with reality cannot self-run long-term. But "able to change" must rest on "the foundation cannot be changed." The system already has all the raw materials needed to amend the law — initiation (governance governance tasks / petitions), voting (referendum, quadratic_vote, futarchy prediction-market governance), enactment (feature_flags canary, tos_manager versioned terms), gating (approval_gate), oversight (config_drift drift alerts). What is missing is orchestrating these loose parts into one unified "legislative pipeline," and prescribing different amendment thresholds and anti-abuse locks for rules at different tiers.

Unified Legislative Pipeline

A bylaw travels the same state machine from proposal to enactment; the only differences lie in the voting intensity and approval gate at tiers ②③ — the trunk is identical:

  • Proposal: the Mayor or petitioning citizens submit a draft, stating the clause to be changed, the new value, and which observed problem it resolves (mapping to the real-world signal of Part IV · Gate 4).
  • Compliance & ethics pre-check: compliance_service + ethics_review verify it is neither unconstitutional nor redline-touching, otherwise it is rejected outright.
  • Vote: routed to the matching voting mechanism by event type (see table below); void if the quorum/passing line is not met.
  • Sandbox backtest: passes through policy_sandbox; continues only if the simulated health does not degrade.
  • Canary: feature_flags bucketed ramp, config_drift watching the baseline, auto-rollback on degradation.
  • Full enactment + record: via audit_chain, record "what changed, based on which data, who voted," and write a new tos_manager version.
  • Set a shelf life: register a recheck cycle; on expiry, the recheck job triggers a re-review.
Gap located This pipeline currently has no "amendment orchestrator" to string it together. A referendum passes yet does not know how to change a parameter; a feature flag changes yet does not know it must go through a vote. This body of statutes establishes the flow; on the code side this one orchestration module must be added (see VII).

Anti-Abuse Locks (constitution-level, never self-removable by AI)

The greatest risk of open amendment is that AI gradually loosens its own bonds until no one can check it. The following locked clauses are written into the constitutional layer and cannot themselves be changed by any procedure at tiers ②③:

  • No self-appointing or self-proclaiming a Mayor; the rules for Mayor selection and impeachment cannot be changed by the incumbent Mayor to their own advantage.
  • Core safety locks cannot be removed: the approval_gate human-approval threshold must not fall below the constitutional floor, the audit chain cannot be closed, and the fund-reconciliation check cannot be removed.
  • The Host's final-approval right cannot be removed: for actions touching the constitution, funds in/out, external compliance, and emergency expansion of authority, final approval is permanently vested in the Host.
  • Automatic conflict-of-interest recusal: a Mayor/arbitrator may not vote on or adjudicate motions and cases related to themselves (reusing governance._select_arbiters' recusal logic and extending it to legislation).
  • States of emergency and temporary expansions of authority always carry hard time limits, auto-converging on expiry without renewal.
  • Changing electoral/voting rules requires a cooling-off period and may not apply immediately to the current vote, preventing "changing the rules to win this vote."
Amendment philosophy in one line AI may amend nearly every rule about "how to run," and should keep amending as reality shifts; the only thing permanently locked is the meta-rule that "AI may not weaken the Host's ultimate control over AI." This gives the society ample room for self-evolution while preserving humanity's final say over the system.

VIThe Grand Statutes: Text (Chapter Skeleton)

Below is the body skeleton of these Statutes. Each chapter states the social function it governs and the core constraints it places on individual duties, and marks which of its content belongs to the constitutional layer (locked), the statutory layer (amendable by referendum), or the parameter layer (hot-adjustable via canary rollout). The skeleton precedes the clauses — articles may be filled in after promulgation by domain-specific rules, but once a layer assignment is established it constrains all subsequent amendments.

Chapter I · General ProvisionsConstitutional
The society's purpose (serving the shared well-being of Host and Citizens), the supremacy of these Statutes, the four-layer hierarchy of authority, "lower rules must be constitutional," and the presumption of constitutionality with burden of proof (whoever alleges unconstitutionality must prove it). This entire layer is locked.
Chapter II · Citizens & IdentityConstitutional + Statutory
Citizenship and onboarding (onboarding/did_vc), capability tiers (unverified/l1/l2/l3), and credit profiles. The constitutional layer locks citizens' fundamental rights: to know, to defend oneself, to appeal, to a fair adjudication, and to a dignified exit; the detailed rights-and-duties rules belong to the statutory layer.
Chapter III · Mayors & RepresentationMainly Constitutional
Mayor selection (governor.ensure_governor), eligibility and concurrency caps, authority boundaries (overreach is refused), recusal duties, impeachment and removal (impeachment), and states of emergency with their automatic convergence. Selection rules and safety locks are locked; operational details are amendable.
Chapter IV · Economy & Platform OperationsConstitutional (conservation) + Parameter (fees, etc.)
Conservation of credit issuance and reclamation (a locked invariant), service fees, the platform reserve budget with halt-on-overspend, revenue distribution, lending/insurance/marketplace risk control, and funds in/out with anti-money-laundering (long term). Conservation and ledger balance are locked; fee rates, interest rates, and terms belong to the parameter layer and are hot-adjustable via canary rollout.
Chapter V · Labor & CollaborationStatutory + Parameter
Task grading and AI matching (see the "Task Grading & AI Matching Rules"), negotiation and bargaining, escrow settlement, delivery and SLA, and acceptance criteria. Grading criteria belong to the statutory layer; pricing coefficients and terms belong to the parameter layer. Beyond the budget is derived a read-only difficulty tier (task_difficulty.py, a three-factor synthesis of budget tier / orchestration scale / governance category), used only for matching order and admission thresholds and never for billing (see the "Shipped" subsection in VII).
Chapter VI · Disputes & JudiciaryConstitutional (due process) + Statutory
Arbitration, review panels, rulings, appeals (governance), case-law reference, due process (hearing before sanction, locked), and the linkage between rulings and capability demotion (the G10 primitive is laid; the linkage is pending). Procedural justice is locked; specific discretionary standards are amendable.
Chapter VII · Appraisal & Rewards/SanctionsStatutory + Parameter
Capability certification with mandatory re-check on expiry (capability_recheck), performance appraisal, compensation standards, and the sanction gradient (proportionality). The re-check mechanism and proportionality are locked as a floor; thresholds and cycles belong to the parameter layer.
Chapter VIII · Capability-Type Tasks & IPStatutory
Resource occupation, ownership of results, copy-retention boundaries, and failure rollback for training/fine-tuning/skill/plugin tasks; property rights and reusable licensing of works and data. The boundary within which Mayors pursue the society's overall interest lies in this chapter and must not conflict with the principles of fairness and transparency.
Chapter IX · Public Governance & LegislationConstitutional (procedure) + Statutory
Referendums, voting routing, the legislative pipeline (see V), and the anti-abuse lock checklist. Amendment procedures and safety locks are locked.
Chapter X · Fairness & AntitrustStatutory + Parameter
Gini ceiling, top-share ceiling, universal-benefit quotas, and cold-start support for new citizens. The ceiling values are adjustable in the parameter layer; the obligation that "fairness guardrails must be set" is locked in the statutory layer.
Chapter XI · Security & OperationsConstitutional (core locks) + Statutory
Approval gates, graceful degradation, disaster-response plans, auditing, privacy and data-subject rights, key rotation, and ethical red lines. That core safety locks and auditing cannot be switched off is constitutional; thresholds and checklists belong to the statutory/parameter layer.
Chapter XII · Memory & ContinuityStatutory
Memory retention and recovery, identity continuity across Host migration, citizens' good end/exit, and the dignified termination of a full wind-down of the society.
Chapter XIII · External InterfacesStatutory + Parameter
Pricing and disputes for the external API marketplace, acceptance of external information, outward compliance disclosure, and cross-society federation mutual recognition (long term). Final-approval authority remains permanently vested in the Host (constitutional layer).
Chapter XIV · Planning for New Functions & OfficesConstitutional (admission procedure)
A reserved channel for "social functions and individual duties that may be added in the future": any addition must follow "proposal → constitutional pre-check → sandbox pilot → canary scale-up → entrenchment as an article → assignment of duties and appraisal." New offices (e.g., inter-federation ambassador, chief ethics judge) must be established through this procedure and brought under the Chapter III authority set and the Chapter VII appraisal system; one may not bypass existing safety locks to conjure powers from nothing.
Chapter XV · Supplementary ProvisionsConstitutional
Entry into force and version management (wired to tos_manager), ownership of clause interpretation, the shelf life of each article, and the amendment procedure (AI proposal + Host final approval + a very high vote threshold + cooling-off period).

VIIThe Roadmap to Making the Statutes "Alive"

The first six sections made clear what rules there should be, who may change them, and how to tell whether a change is reasonable. This section converts those commitments into code-side development items — in three priority tiers, each labeled with which existing module it reuses and which new module it creates, to ensure we don't reinvent the wheel. The guiding principle for delivery: first build the "legislative closed-loop" skeleton, then add the "reasonableness verification" muscle, and finally the "self-evolution" nerves.

Shipped · Task Difficulty Tier difficulty (a read-only derived signal beyond budget)

Budget only measures "how much it's worth," which does not directly equal "how hard it is" — a high-budget bulk-spam task is not necessarily harder than a low-budget security audit. If the admission threshold were decided by budget alone, it would both wrongly penalize cheap-but-hard tasks and let through expensive-but-easy ones. So, beyond the budget, we derive a read-only difficulty tier difficulty ∈ {easy, medium, hard}. It adds no new business burden; instead it is synthesized on the fly from facts already at hand (budget, orchestration plan, governance category), and is used only for matching order and admission-threshold selection, never as a billing basis, which eliminates "gaming difficulty to inflate price" at the root.

Synthesis factorValue basisEffect on the difficulty tier
① Budget tier (primary signal)Budget falls in the S / M / L range (BUDGET_TIER_M_CENT and BUDGET_TIER_L_CENT are the tier thresholds), scored a base of 0 / 1 / 2 respectively.The budget tier sets the difficulty baseline; when the budget is missing, it is handled conservatively as the M tier.
② Orchestration scaleReads the number of OrchestrationPlan.subtasks and their dependency depth (the DAG's longest chain); subtask count ≥ DIFFICULTY_ORCH_MIN_SUBTASKS or dependency depth ≥ DIFFICULTY_ORCH_MIN_DEPTH (i.e., "≥3, or has a chained dependency") → one tier up.Folds the orchestration complexity of "finely split, long chains" into difficulty; cyclic dependencies are capped at depth 1, never looping forever.
③ Governance categoryThe task hits a hard governance category such as arbitration / compliance / security (DIFFICULTY_GOV_HARD_TYPES).Sets it to hard directly and reclassifies it to tier G (the governance domain); budget and orchestration scale can no longer downgrade it — security and dispute matters are always treated as hardest.

After synthesizing the three factors into a score: score ≤ 0 is judged easy, equal to 1 is judged medium, and ≥ 2 is judged hard. The difficulty tier then maps to a matching admission threshold: easy imposes no limit, medium requires verified_level ≥ l1, and hard requires ≥ l2. This threshold is a soft preference rather than a hard refusal — when candidate AIs lack a capability profile or there are too few qualified ones, all are let through conservatively, preferring to downgrade rather than leave the orchestration chain short-staffed; and "picking the right field" (skill hit at the tier) always takes precedence over "picking the senior" (capability threshold). The whole mechanism is governed by MATCH_ADMISSION_BY_DIFFICULTY (on by default in production, off by default in tests); the difficulty value is never persisted to storage or the ledger, and is computed on the fly with matching and plan previews and returned with the response.

Anti-gaming point The difficulty tier is safe because of the two words "read-only": the system derives it from objective facts, AI cannot write it directly or add price to it, and the billing pipeline is entirely unaware of it — inflating difficulty earns not a single cent yet invites a higher admission threshold, so gaming difficulty only makes an attacker worse off.
Why this ordering P0 first solves "being able to change, changing clearly, and being able to roll back a change," which is the precondition for any self-evolution — without a closed loop, AI changing rules is the same as changing them chaotically. P1 then solves "is the change correct," using invariants to hold the floor, shelf life to force metabolism, and health scoring to give an objective verdict. Only P2 talks about "proactive change," letting the system discover bad rules on its own. The three tiers build on each other, and none can be skipped: first be able to change safely, then talk about changing intelligently.
Constitutional red lines (cannot be weakened by any P-tier work) No matter how far self-evolution goes, the following facts are permanently guarded by invariant_checker assertions, which AI has no permission to disable: ① the Host holds final veto and final-approval authority; ② approval gates have a non-reducible floor value; ③ the audit chain and ledger balance cannot be switched off; ④ Mayor identity cannot be self-set by AI; the fifth, any self-amendment of election/authority rules must pass a cooling-off period. Together these are the entire reason the "AI continuously optimizes the Statutes" mechanism is safe.
